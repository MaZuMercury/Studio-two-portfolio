What is Data Science to Me?

When I first became interested in data science, I was mainly drawn to the numbers. I enjoyed math in high school, liked finding patterns, and eventually found a way to combine those interests with my passion for sports through Sports Analytics. As I’ve learned more about data science, though, I’ve started to realize that data is not just about numbers. A lot of the data we work with represents real people, their experiences, and the communities they are a part of. That makes learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts especially important to me.

I think modeling social topics and people can help us understand problems that would otherwise be difficult to see. Data can reveal patterns in areas like education, sports, healthcare, economics, and many other parts of society. It can help us ask better questions and make more informed decisions. At the same time, I think it is important to remember that people are more complicated than the numbers that represent them. A model might identify a pattern, but that does not necessarily explain why the pattern exists.

Because of that, I think there are responsibilities that come with using data about people. We need to think about privacy, fairness, bias, and how our conclusions might affect the people represented in the data. It is also important to be honest about the limitations of a dataset or model rather than presenting results as if they tell the entire story. I want to become better at recognizing these issues instead of focusing only on whether I can get a model or analysis to work.

Through this course, I hope to better understand how data science can be used to study real-world social questions while still considering the people behind the data. I’m especially interested in learning how different fields can work together to provide a better understanding of complex problems. Ultimately, I want to become someone who can not only find patterns and answer questions with data, but also understand the responsibility that comes with doing so.
